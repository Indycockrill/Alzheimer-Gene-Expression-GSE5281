# Alzheimer-Gene-Expression-GSE5281
## Goal
I wanted to identify genes that are significantly up- or downregulated in Alzheimer’s disease neurons compared with healthy control neurons and understand what those changes might mean biologically.

## Dataset
I used the publicly available dataset GSE5281 from the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus. The data contains gene expression measurements from laser-captured neurons taken from Alzheimer’s patients and age-matched non-demented controls.

## Methods
I performed the analysis with the GEO2R tool. I defined two groups (AD and Control) and ran a differential expression analysis using the default settings. Genes were ranked by statistical significance (–log10 p-value) and fold change. I then selected the strongest genes for further interpretation.

## Key Findings
The strongest upregulated genes included NEAT1, ITPKB, and ACACB. NEAT1 is a long non-coding RNA linked to stress responses and Alzheimer’s pathology, while ITPKB is involved in calcium signaling. ACACB helps regulate fatty-acid oxidation. Several genes related to energy production (ATP5B and ATP5C1) and cytoskeletal structure (TUBB) were downregulated, along with YWHAZ, a protein that interacts with tau. Overall, these changes are consistent with known problems in Alzheimer’s neurons, including reduced energy production, weakened internal structure, and altered signaling.

## Conclusion
This analysis shows that public transcriptomic data can reveal biologically meaningful patterns in Alzheimer’s disease. It also demonstrates the process of moving from raw gene-expression results to a clear biological interpretation.
