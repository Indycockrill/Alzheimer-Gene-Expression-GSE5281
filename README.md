# Differential Gene Expression Analysis in Alzheimer’s Disease Neurons (GSE5281)

## Goal
Identify genes that are significantly up- or downregulated in laser-captured neurons from Alzheimer’s disease patients compared with healthy controls, and interpret the biological meaning of the strongest changes.

## Dataset
This analysis uses the publicly available dataset **GSE5281** from the NCBI Gene Expression Omnibus:  
[https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE5281](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE5281)

The dataset contains gene expression profiles from laser-captured layer III pyramidal neurons across multiple brain regions. It includes approximately 87 Alzheimer’s disease samples and 74 non-demented control samples, generated on the Affymetrix Human Genome U133 Plus 2.0 Array.

## Methods
Differential expression analysis was performed using the GEO2R tool with default limma settings. Samples were grouped as “AD” versus “Control.” Results were ranked by statistical significance (–log10 p-value) and log2 fold change. The strongest and most biologically relevant genes were selected for interpretation.

## Key Findings
The strongest upregulated genes included **NEAT1**, **ITPKB**, and **ACACB**.  
- NEAT1 is a long non-coding RNA linked to cellular stress responses and Alzheimer’s pathology.  
- ITPKB is involved in calcium signaling and has been associated with amyloid and tau pathways.  
- ACACB regulates mitochondrial fatty-acid oxidation.

Several genes were significantly downregulated:  
- **ATP5B** and **ATP5C1** (subunits of mitochondrial ATP synthase) — indicating reduced capacity for cellular energy production.  
- **TUBB** (beta-tubulin) — a core component of microtubules, suggesting cytoskeletal disruption.  
- **YWHAZ** (14-3-3 zeta) — a signaling protein known to interact with tau.

A summary table of these top genes is available here: [top-genes.md](top-genes.md)

## Biological Interpretation
The overall expression pattern is consistent with several well-established features of Alzheimer’s disease. Downregulation of ATP synthase subunits points to mitochondrial energy failure, while reduced TUBB expression aligns with the known breakdown of microtubule structure and tau-related pathology. Upregulation of NEAT1, ITPKB, and ACACB further suggests altered stress responses, calcium signaling, and lipid metabolism. Together, these changes paint a coherent picture of neurons under metabolic and structural stress.

## Limitations
This is an exploratory analysis using GEO2R default settings. Results should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive. No additional custom filtering or pathway enrichment was performed beyond the standard GEO2R output.

## Conclusion
This project demonstrates the process of working with public transcriptomic data, performing differential expression analysis, and connecting statistical results to biological context in Alzheimer’s disease.
